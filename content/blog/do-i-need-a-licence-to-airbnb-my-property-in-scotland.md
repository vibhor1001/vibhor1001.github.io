**Short answer:** Yes, in most cases. Scotland's [short-term let licensing scheme](https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets/) covers Airbnb-style accommodation such as spare rooms, holiday cottages and guest houses, and new hosts generally need a licence before accepting bookings or receiving guests.

**If you're wondering whether you need a licence to run an Airbnb in Scotland, this guide covers the exceptions, the four licence types, what it costs and how to apply.**

## Do I need a licence to Airbnb my property in Scotland?

Yes. The Scottish Government says a licence is required [regardless of how long you let the accommodation](https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets/), whether that's one night or several months. **It covers holiday cottages, B&amp;Bs, guest houses, rooms in your own home, and even pods and yurts.**

New hosts have needed one since 1 October 2022, and you must hold it before you accept a booking or receive guests. Hosts who were already trading and applied before 1 October 2023 could continue operating while their council decided on the application. If you're still in this situation, check with your local council about the status of your application.

What about letting for just a few weekends a year? You still need permission. There's no minimum number of nights. But [some councils offer temporary licences or exemptions](https://www.mygov.scot/short-term-let-licences/types), up to six weeks a year, so check yours.

## Who doesn't need a licence?

**The common ones are:**

* guests who are close family, part-owners, or staying to work for you
* anyone on a tenancy, such as a private residential tenancy
* hotels with planning permission as a hotel
* aparthotels, hostels, bothys, and boats or yachts that can be moved
* accommodation already licensed for overnight stays, such as a holiday caravan site, or a premises whose alcohol licence covers the accommodation

If none of those fit, assume you need one. **The government's** **[licence checker](https://www.mygov.scot/check-short-term-let-licence)** **takes a couple of minutes**.

## Which licence do you need?

![Graphic explaining the four Scottish short-term-let licence types: home sharing, home letting, home sharing and home letting, and secondary letting.](/images/blog/word/do-i-need-a-licence-to-airbnb-my-property-in-scotland/2.webp)

*(Scotland has four short-term-let licence types, depending on whether you live in the property and how you let it)*

There are [four types](https://www.mygov.scot/short-term-let-licences/types), depending on whether you live in the property:

| **Licence**                   | **When you need it**                         |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Home sharing                  | You let part of your home while you're there |
| Home letting                  | You let your home while you're away          |
| Home letting and home sharing | You do both                                  |
| Secondary letting             | A property you don't normally live in        |

**Most Airbnb flats and cottages fall under secondary letting.**

## Three quick examples

* **The spare room.** Say you rent out a room in your Glasgow flat while you're at home. That's home sharing, so you need a licence.
* **The holiday cottage.** You own a cottage in Fife that you don't live in. That's secondary letting, typically the dearest licence.
* **The festival month.** You live in Edinburgh and let your own flat only in August. You still need permission, but a temporary licence or exemption may cost less than a full licence where the council offers one. Edinburgh does.

A guest house is covered too. There's no separate guest house licence, because the same scheme applies unless an exclusion fits, such as an alcohol licence that covers the accommodation.

## How much does a licence to Airbnb my property in Scotland cost?

The council sets the fee, so it varies a lot. mygov.scot says to [check with your local council](https://www.mygov.scot/short-term-let-licences/apply). **As one example, Edinburgh's** **[published fees](https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/licences-permits/short-term-let-licence-application-fees-202324)** **for 2025–26 are:**

| **Licence**                          | **Edinburgh fee**                                           |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Home sharing                         | £120                                                        |
| Home letting                         | £120 per occupant (new), £360 per occupant (3-year renewal) |
| Secondary letting, 1 to 3 occupants  | £653 (1 year)                                               |
| Secondary letting, 4 to 5 occupants  | £1,089 (1 year)                                             |
| Secondary letting, 6 to 10 occupants | £2,481 (1 year)                                             |
| Secondary letting, 21 or more        | £5,869 (1 year)                                             |

Other councils charge differently, so your own council's page is the only reliable number. Budget for the certificates and insurance on top of the fee.

## What you need before you apply

![Checklist of key requirements for a Scottish short-term-let licence, including gas safety, EICR, PAT testing, insurance, alarms, Legionella assessment and EPC.](/images/blog/word/do-i-need-a-licence-to-airbnb-my-property-in-scotland/3.webp)

*(The key safety, insurance and property documents to prepare before applying for a Scottish short-term-let licence)*

Your accommodation must meet the [mandatory conditions](https://www.mygov.scot/short-term-let-licences/legal-requirements). In practice you'll need:

* a Gas Safety Certificate dated within the last 12 months
* an Electrical Installation Condition Report
* a Portable Appliance Testing report for appliances guests can use
* buildings insurance and public liability insurance
* working smoke, heat and carbon monoxide alarms, and a fire risk assessment
* a Legionella risk assessment
* an Energy Performance Certificate dated within the last 10 years, if you let a whole house or flat

Councils can add conditions, and some ask for floor plans.

## How to apply for an Airbnb licence online

1. **Check you need one**, and which type, with the [government checker](https://www.mygov.scot/check-short-term-let-licence).
2. **Gather your certificates** and any floor plan your council asks for.
3. **Apply on your council's website.** mygov.scot lists a [link for every council](https://www.mygov.scot/short-term-let-licences/apply).
4. **Display a public notice** at or near the property for 21 days from the date you apply.
5. **Wait for a decision.** Councils have 9 months, and can ask the sheriff court for more time.
6. **Once granted,** include your licence number in every advert, and keep a certified copy of the licence and your safety information where guests can access it.

If your application is refused, you can appeal to the sheriff court within 28 days. You can't reapply for a year unless your circumstances materially change.

## Planning permission and other Airbnb rules in Scotland

A licence isn't planning permission. Councils can designate control areas, and so far that's [Edinburgh (since 5 September 2022) and Highland's Badenoch and Strathspey ward (since 4 March 2024)](https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets/). In a control area, letting a secondary home can need planning permission, and you must have it, or have applied, before you apply for the licence.

Edinburgh also introduced a [5% visitor levy on 24 July 2026](https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/news/article/14141/edinburgh-declares-scotland-s-first-visitor-levy) on the first five consecutive nights of a stay, and accommodation providers collect it.

## Licence to Airbnb my property in Scotland: buying or selling

Licences don't pass automatically with a sale. The seller can apply to [transfer the licence](https://www.mygov.scot/short-term-let-licences/apply) to the buyer, who should contact the council. Edinburgh charges £120 for a transfer.

If you're buying, check the [public register](https://www.mygov.scot/check-short-term-let-register) and ask whether planning permission applies. If you're building a new place, you can apply for a provisional licence, which shows lenders you're likely to get a full one.

## What happens if you don't have a licence?

It's a criminal offence. The [maximum fine is £2,500](https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets/pages/enforcement/), and a prosecuted host can be disqualified from holding a licence for a year. Your insurance and lending arrangements can also be affected.

The government calls enforcement a last resort, but councils publish registers, so neighbours can check.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a licence to Airbnb my property in Scotland?**

Yes. Almost every short-term let needs one, however many nights you let.

**How long does a licence take?**

Councils have up to 9 months to process an application.

**Do I need a licence for a spare room?**

Yes. That's a home sharing licence.

**Do I have to show the licence number on my listing?**

Yes. Once you have a licence, include the number in your adverts. (You'll also see it spelled "licence" or "license" depending on the site.)

**Is a licence the same as planning permission?**

No. They're separate, and in a control area you may need both.
