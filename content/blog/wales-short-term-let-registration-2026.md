If you let a property in Wales, the rules have changed. Wales short term let registration is now a legal requirement, and the register is open.

If you take bookings for overnight stays in visitor accommodation in Wales, and any of those bookings are for 31 nights or less, you generally need to register with the [Welsh Revenue Authority](https://www.gov.wales/register-your-visitor-accommodation-wales) (WRA).

This guide covers who must register, how to register online, the deadline and penalties, and how registration differs from the visitor levy and licensing. If you run short term lets in Wales, whether one cottage or a portfolio, it should answer the questions you have before you start.

## What Is Welsh Registration for Short-Term Lets?

The Welsh registration scheme is a national register of visitor accommodation, run by the WRA. It comes from the Visitor Accommodation (Register and Levy) Etc. (Wales) Act 2025, and it applies to all visitor accommodation, not just short term rentals.

[Registration is free](https://www.gov.wales/registering-visitor-accommodation-overview), and it applies even if your local council never introduces a Visitor Levy. It is a legal requirement, and penalties for failing to comply apply from 1 April 2027.

## Who Must Register?

**You must register if all of the following apply:**

* You take bookings for overnight stays in visitor accommodation in Wales, whether for one night, seasonally or year-round.
* You are responsible for the accommodation, for example you own or rent it.
* Any of the bookings you take are for 31 nights or less.

**The** **[WRA guidance](https://www.gov.wales/registering-visitor-accommodation-overview)** **lists the types covered:**

* self-catering lets such as cottages, houses, apartments or lodges
* your main home or spare rooms
* hotels, bed and breakfasts and guesthouses
* pitches, glamping and static caravans
* hostels and bunkhouses

That means Wales short term rental hosts on Airbnb, Booking.com or Vrbo are included. The guidance gives the example of a host who advertises her main home on Airbnb once a year for a local event: she must register it.

**You do not need to register if you do not take bookings, for example when friends or family stay for free, or if you have stopped taking bookings for overnight stays**.

## How to Register Online

Wales short term let registration online runs through a [WRA service on GOV.WALES](https://www.gov.wales/register-your-visitor-accommodation-wales). The [Welsh Government says](https://www.gov.wales/get-ready-register-your-visitor-accommodation-wales) it takes most people under 15 minutes.

1. **Sign in.** You use GOV.UK One Login, and you are asked to create an account if you don't have one.
2. **Enter your details.** Have these ready:

   * your name, address, phone number, email and date of birth
   * a company or charity number and trading name, if applicable
   * the address and name of the accommodation
   * the types of accommodation you offer
   * when it is usually open for bookings
   * the number of rooms or bookable units
   * how many people can stay
3. **Get your registration number.** After registering you receive a unique registration number, which you can use to show your accommodation is registered and to contact the WRA about your registration.

If you cannot use the online service, you can call the WRA helpdesk on **03000 254 000**, Monday to Friday, 10am to 3pm. The WRA also runs [free training and events](https://www.gov.wales/welsh-revenue-authority/training-events), and you can [contact the WRA](https://www.gov.wales/welsh-revenue-authority/contact-us) for help.

## If You Have More Than One Property

**If the same people or organisations are responsible for every premises, you must include all of them in one registration.** If different people or organisations are responsible for different premises, you register them separately.

"Premises" means each address you offer accommodation at. Three houses at three addresses are three premises. A converted barn and a cottage at the same address are one.

## If You Use an Agent or Management Company

The accommodation provider, which is you, is responsible for registering. [Agents or management companies can register on your behalf](https://www.gov.wales/registering-visitor-accommodation-overview), but the responsibility does not move to them. Confirm in writing who is doing what so a registration is not missed.

## What Becomes Public

Some of your [details](https://www.gov.wales/viewing-visitor-accommodation-details-public-register) will appear on the public register. This includes your registration number, provider or company name, trading name, business address and accommodation details such as the property address, accommodation type, availability, number of rooms or spaces and maximum number of guests. **Your date of birth, phone number and email address are not shown publicly.**

## What Are the Penalties?

![Example of Wales short-term-let registration penalties for three premises, showing £100 per premises, £10 per premises per day for 30 days, and a further £1,000 per premises, totalling £4,200.](/images/blog/word/wales-short-term-let-registration-2026/3.webp)

*(The Welsh Revenue Authority gives an example where three unregistered premises can result in total penalties of £4,200 if registration is still not completed after the initial penalty period)*

**The** **[penalty for failing to register](https://www.gov.wales/failing-register-update-details-or-deregister-your-visitor-accommodation), update your details or deregister is £100 per premises.** It applies per address, and you must pay within 30 days.

If you still have not registered after getting a penalty:

* a further £10 per premises per day applies for up to the next 30 days
* if you still do not register in that time, a further £1,000 per premises applies

The WRA can also register you itself. That counts as a late registration, and the same penalties apply. The GOV.WALES example shows a host with three houses in Cardiff who ends up with a total of £4,200 in penalties. You can [ask for a review or appeal](https://www.gov.wales/disagree-wra-tax-decision/request-review-tax-decision-penalty) if you think a penalty is wrong.

## Keeping Your Registration Up to Date

You must update your registration within 30 days if anything changes. This includes selling the accommodation, no longer taking bookings, or changes to contact details, accommodation details or who is responsible. If you stop offering your accommodation, you must deregister within 30 days of the last guest leaving.

At the time of writing, the [registration service](https://www.gov.wales/register-your-visitor-accommodation-wales) says that updating, removing accommodation and adding people to help manage your registration will be added "later in the year." Until then, contact the WRA within 30 days of a change.

## Registration vs Visitor Levy vs Licensing

![Comparison of Wales short-term-let registration, Visitor Levy and licensing, showing their current status, key dates and requirements.](/images/blog/word/wales-short-term-let-registration-2026/4.webp)

*(Registration, the Visitor Levy and visitor accommodation licensing are separate parts of Wales' short-term-let regulatory framework)*

These are three separate things, and hosts often mix them up.

**Registration** is live now. It applies to all visitor accommodation, and it is free.

**Visitor Levy** is a charge that councils can choose to introduce. It starts [no earlier than April 2027](https://www.gov.wales/visitor-levy). If your council introduces the levy, the WRA expects you to [keep good records](https://www.gov.wales/visitor-levy-accommodation-providers-steps/good-records). Keeping each booking's statement and payout tied to the stay, as [PropertyFlow's financials](https://www.propertyflow.uk/platform/financials/) do, makes that easier.

**Licensing** is not required yet. The [Development of Tourism and Regulation of Visitor Accommodation (Wales) Act 2026](https://www.gov.wales/development-tourism-and-regulation-visitor-accommodation-wales-act-2026) received Royal Assent on 27 April 2026. Licensing will start with self-catering accommodation. The Welsh Government expects licensing to begin in 2029, while the legal default is that the provisions come into force by March 2030, after further consultation on the regulations and published guidance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**1. Do I have to register my short term let in Wales?**

Yes, if you take bookings for overnight stays in Wales and any booking is for 31 nights or less.

**2. What is the deadline for Wales short term let registration?**

Register all premises already offered for bookings by 31 March 2027. From 1 April 2027, register before you open for bookings.

**3. Is Welsh registration free?**

Yes. Registration is free, but penalties apply if you fail to register, update or deregister on time.

**4. Can I register on behalf of someone else?**

Agents and management companies can register on your behalf, but you remain the responsible provider.

**5. Is registration the same as a licence?**

No. Registration is required now. Licensing is a separate scheme that is planned for later.

**6. Will I pay the visitor levy?**

Only if your local council introduces it, and not before April 2027.
